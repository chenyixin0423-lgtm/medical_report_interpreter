Indicator: LYMPH (Lymphocytes)
Full name: Lymphocyte Count / Lymphocyte Percentage

Reference range:
- Percentage (LYMPH%): 20 % – 40 %
- Absolute count (LYMPH#): 1.0 – 3.3 × 10⁹/L
Unit: % or × 10⁹/L

Clinical significance:
Lymphocytes are the immune system's key coordinators: T cells mediate cellular immunity (killing virus-infected cells and tumour cells) and B cells produce antibodies. They are central to adaptive immunity against viral infections and long-term immune memory.

HIGH (lymphocytosis) — common causes:
- Viral infections (most common — influenza, COVID-19, EBV, CMV)
- Pertussis (whooping cough — can cause striking lymphocytosis)
- Tuberculosis
- Lymphocytic leukaemia (consider when LYMPH# is markedly and persistently elevated)
- Normal in infants and young children (lymphocytes dominate in early childhood)

LOW (lymphocytopenia, LYMPH# < 1.0 × 10⁹/L) — common causes:
- Long-term corticosteroid use
- Chemotherapy or radiotherapy
- HIV / AIDS (progressive destruction of CD4⁺ T cells)
- Severe sepsis
- Autoimmune diseases
- Severe malnutrition

Key diagnostic pattern:
- Bacterial infection: neutrophils ↑, lymphocyte percentage relatively ↓
- Viral infection: lymphocyte percentage ↑, neutrophil percentage relatively ↓
This "shift" helps distinguish bacterial from viral aetiology on a routine CBC.

Notes:
- A persistently low lymphocyte count warrants HIV testing and immune function evaluation.
- Absolute lymphocyte count (not just percentage) should be used when bone marrow suppression is suspected.
