Indicator: TBIL (Total Bilirubin)
Full name: Total Bilirubin

Reference range: 3.4 – 17.1 µmol/L  (0.2 – 1.0 mg/dL)
Unit: µmol/L or mg/dL

Clinical significance:
Bilirubin is the yellow breakdown product of haem (from ageing red blood cells). It is processed by the liver and excreted in bile. Total bilirubin = direct (conjugated) bilirubin + indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin. When bilirubin accumulates, it causes jaundice — yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes.

Jaundice grading:
- Latent / pre-clinical: 17.1 – 34.2 µmol/L   (not visible to the naked eye)
- Mild jaundice:         34.2 – 85.5 µmol/L
- Moderate jaundice:    85.5 – 171  µmol/L
- Severe jaundice:      > 171 µmol/L

HIGH — classified by predominant fraction:

Predominantly indirect bilirubin elevated (IBIL ↑):
- Haemolytic anaemia (accelerated RBC destruction)
- Neonatal jaundice (physiological or pathological)
- Gilbert's syndrome (benign hereditary condition — often an incidental finding)
- Ineffective erythropoiesis

Predominantly direct bilirubin elevated (DBIL ↑):
- Biliary obstruction (gallstones, tumours blocking bile ducts)
- Hepatocellular disease (viral hepatitis, cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma)
- Drug-induced cholestasis

LOW: no clinical significance.

Notes:
- Mildly elevated TBIL (< 34 µmol/L) with all other liver tests normal is very often Gilbert's syndrome — a harmless genetic variant requiring no treatment.
- Neonatal jaundice requires close monitoring to prevent kernicterus (bilirubin-induced brain damage).
- Combine with DBIL/IBIL ratio and ALT/ALP pattern to classify jaundice type (haemolytic / hepatocellular / obstructive) before further workup.
