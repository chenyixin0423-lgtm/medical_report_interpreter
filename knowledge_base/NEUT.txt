Indicator: NEUT (Neutrophils)
Full name: Neutrophil Count / Neutrophil Percentage

Reference range:
- Percentage (NEUT%): 50 % – 70 %
- Absolute count (NEUT#): 1.8 – 6.3 × 10⁹/L
Unit: % or × 10⁹/L

Clinical significance:
Neutrophils are the most abundant white blood cells and the immune system's first responders against bacterial infection. They engulf and destroy bacteria within minutes of arriving at an infection site.

HIGH (neutrophilia) — common causes:
- Bacterial infection (classic indicator, e.g. pneumonia, appendicitis, cellulitis)
- Acute inflammation (appendicitis, cholecystitis)
- Tissue injury (trauma, surgery, myocardial infarction)
- Corticosteroid therapy (e.g. prednisone)
- Granulocytic leukaemia (consider when markedly elevated)
- Physiological: intense exercise, emotional stress, late pregnancy

LOW (neutropenia, NEUT# < 1.8 × 10⁹/L) — common causes:
- Viral infections (viruses often suppress neutrophils while lymphocytes rise)
- Chemotherapy or radiation therapy (most common cause in treated cancer patients)
- Certain drugs (sulfonamides, methimazole, clozapine)
- Autoimmune diseases
- Bone marrow failure

Critical thresholds (absolute neutrophil count):
- < 1.0 × 10⁹/L : Significantly increased infection risk (febrile neutropenia)
- < 0.5 × 10⁹/L : Severe agranulocytosis — risk of life-threatening infection

Notes:
- Elevated neutrophils with fever strongly suggest a bacterial infection.
- When neutrophils are low, patients should practise strict hand hygiene and avoid crowded places.
- Combine with lymphocyte count, CRP, and temperature to distinguish bacterial from viral infection.
