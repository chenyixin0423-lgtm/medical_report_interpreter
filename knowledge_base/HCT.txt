Indicator: HCT / PCV (Hematocrit / Packed Cell Volume)
Full name: Hematocrit

Reference range:
- Adult males:   0.40 – 0.50  (40 % – 50 %)
- Adult females: 0.35 – 0.45  (35 % – 45 %)
Unit: dimensionless fraction or percentage (%)

Clinical significance:
Hematocrit is the proportion of whole blood volume occupied by red blood cells — essentially a measure of how "thick" or concentrated the blood is. It is a quick indicator of anaemia and haemoconcentration.

HIGH — common causes:
- Dehydration (most common — haemoconcentration from fluid loss)
- High-altitude residence
- Chronic hypoxic lung or heart disease
- Polycythaemia vera
- Severe sweating or diarrhoea

LOW — common causes:
- All forms of anaemia (HCT typically mirrors HGB changes)
- Large-volume IV fluid administration (haemodilution)
- Pregnancy (expanded plasma volume causes relative decrease)
- Acute or chronic blood loss

Notes:
- HCT and HGB usually change in the same direction; a discrepancy may indicate a specimen or measurement issue.
- HCT is used to calculate other red-cell indices (e.g. MCV = HCT / RBC × 10).
- A high HCT combined with headache, flushed face, or visual disturbance warrants evaluation for polycythaemia.
