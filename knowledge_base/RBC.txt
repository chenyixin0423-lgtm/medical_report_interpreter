Indicator: RBC (Red Blood Cell Count)
Full name: Red Blood Cell Count

Reference range:
- Adult males: 4.3 – 5.8 × 10¹²/L
- Adult females: 3.8 – 5.1 × 10¹²/L
- Children: 3.8 – 5.5 × 10¹²/L (varies with age)
Unit: × 10¹²/L (equivalent to 10⁶/µL or M/µL)

Clinical significance:
Red blood cells (erythrocytes) carry oxygen from the lungs to every tissue in the body. The RBC count directly influences the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity.

HIGH — common causes:
- Dehydration (haemoconcentration — a relative increase)
- High-altitude residence (compensatory erythrocytosis)
- Chronic hypoxic conditions (COPD, cyanotic congenital heart disease)
- Polycythaemia vera (bone marrow overproduces RBCs)
- Heavy smoking

LOW — common causes:
- Anaemia of various types (iron-deficiency, haemolytic, aplastic, etc.)
- Acute or chronic blood loss (trauma, GI bleeding, heavy menstruation)
- Chronic kidney disease (reduced erythropoietin production)
- Bone marrow disorders
- Nutritional deficiency (iron, vitamin B12, or folate)

Notes:
- RBC is usually interpreted together with HGB and HCT to characterise the severity of anaemia.
- A low RBC alone does not confirm anaemia — HGB is the definitive parameter.
- Mild fluctuations around menstruation are normal in females.
