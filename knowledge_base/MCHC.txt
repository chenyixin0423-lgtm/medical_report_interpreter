Indicator: MCHC (Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration)
Full name: Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration

Reference range: 320 – 360 g/L
Unit: g/L

Clinical significance:
MCHC is the concentration of hemoglobin within a given volume of packed red blood cells. It reflects how "densely packed" each cell is with hemoglobin and is the most stable of the four red-cell indices.

HIGH (MCHC > 360 g/L) — common causes:
- Hereditary spherocytosis (abnormally shaped RBCs with high hemoglobin density)
- Haemolysed specimen (artefactual — specimen must be recollected)
Note: MCHC > 380 g/L is physiologically impossible in vivo and almost certainly indicates in vitro haemolysis.

LOW (MCHC < 320 g/L) — common causes:
- Iron-deficiency anaemia (hemoglobin synthesis is reduced; cells appear pale)
- Thalassaemia
- Sideroblastic anaemia

Notes:
- MCHC is more resistant to dilution effects than other indices.
- A markedly elevated MCHC (> 380 g/L) almost always means the specimen haemolysed during collection or transport — a repeat blood draw is required before clinical interpretation.
- Interpret MCHC together with MCV and MCH to classify anaemia type accurately.
