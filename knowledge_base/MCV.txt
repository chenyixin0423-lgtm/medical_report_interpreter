Indicator: MCV (Mean Corpuscular Volume)
Full name: Mean Corpuscular Volume

Reference range: 80 – 100 fL
Unit: fL (femtolitres)

Clinical significance:
MCV is the average size of a single red blood cell. It is the key parameter for classifying anaemia into three morphological types: macrocytic (large cells), normocytic (normal-sized cells), and microcytic (small cells).

HIGH — macrocytic anaemia (MCV > 100 fL) — common causes:
- Vitamin B12 deficiency (vegans, patients with chronic gastric disease)
- Folate deficiency (pregnant women, heavy drinkers)
- Alcoholic liver disease (alcohol directly impairs RBC maturation)
- Hypothyroidism
- Certain drugs (methotrexate, hydroxyurea, some antiretrovirals)
- Myelodysplastic syndrome

LOW — microcytic anaemia (MCV < 80 fL) — common causes:
- Iron-deficiency anaemia (most common cause worldwide)
- Thalassaemia (hereditary haemoglobin disorder — cells are characteristically small)
- Sideroblastic anaemia (impaired iron utilisation)
- Some cases of anaemia of chronic disease

NORMAL — normocytic anaemia (MCV 80–100 fL) — causes include:
- Acute blood loss
- Haemolytic anaemia
- Aplastic anaemia
- Chronic kidney disease

Notes:
- MCV should be interpreted together with MCH, MCHC, RDW, serum ferritin, and iron studies.
- Concurrent iron deficiency and B12/folate deficiency can produce a falsely normal MCV.
- Thalassaemia trait gives a low MCV but near-normal HGB — distinguish from iron-deficiency anaemia with iron studies or haemoglobin electrophoresis.
