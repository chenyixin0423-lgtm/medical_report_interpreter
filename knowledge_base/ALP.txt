Indicator: ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase)
Full name: Alkaline Phosphatase

Reference range:
- Adult males:              40 – 130 U/L
- Adult females:            35 – 105 U/L
- Children and adolescents: up to 300–500 U/L (normal during bone growth)
- Pregnancy (third trimester): up to 2–3× the normal adult range (placental ALP)
Unit: U/L

Clinical significance:
ALP is produced by the liver (bile duct cells), bone, intestine, and placenta. Elevated levels mainly indicate biliary tract disease or bone disorders.

HIGH — hepatobiliary origin:
- Biliary obstruction / cholestasis — ALP is the most sensitive routine marker of bile-duct blockage
- Gallstones obstructing the common bile duct
- Cholangitis (bile duct infection)
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis / primary biliary cholangitis
- Liver metastases (secondary tumours in the liver)
- Infiltrative liver disease (sarcoidosis, lymphoma)

HIGH — bone origin:
- Normal rapid bone growth in children and adolescents
- Fracture healing
- Osteoporosis, Paget's disease of bone (osteitis deformans)
- Bone metastases from cancer
- Hyperparathyroidism

HIGH — physiological:
- Third-trimester pregnancy (placental isoform)
- Post-meal (transient intestinal isoform rise)

LOW — common causes:
- Hypothyroidism
- Pernicious anaemia (vitamin B12 deficiency)
- Zinc deficiency
- Hypophosphatasia (rare genetic condition)

Key diagnostic patterns:
- ALP ↑ + GGT ↑       → biliary / cholestatic liver disease
- ALP ↑, GGT normal   → bone source likely (confirm with bone-specific ALP isoenzyme)

Notes:
- Children naturally have ALP values well above the adult reference range — this is a normal finding during growth.
- An isolated ALP elevation in a pregnant woman in the third trimester is almost always from the placenta and is benign.
