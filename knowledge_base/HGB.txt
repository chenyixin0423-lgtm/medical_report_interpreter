Indicator: HGB / HB (Hemoglobin)
Full name: Hemoglobin

Reference range:
- Adult males: 130 – 175 g/L
- Adult females: 115 – 150 g/L
- Pregnant women: ≥ 110 g/L
- Children: 110 – 160 g/L (varies with age)
Unit: g/L (some reports use g/dL; conversion: g/L ÷ 10 = g/dL)

Clinical significance:
Hemoglobin is the protein inside red blood cells responsible for binding and transporting oxygen. It is the primary indicator for diagnosing and grading anaemia.

Anaemia grading (adults):
- Mild anaemia:      HGB 90 g/L to lower limit of normal
- Moderate anaemia: HGB 60 – 90 g/L
- Severe anaemia:   HGB 30 – 60 g/L
- Very severe:      HGB < 30 g/L

HIGH — common causes:
- Dehydration (haemoconcentration)
- High-altitude residence or chronic hypoxia
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
- Polycythaemia vera

LOW (anaemia) — common causes:
- Iron-deficiency anaemia (most common, especially in women and children)
- Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (megaloblastic anaemia)
- Haemolytic anaemia (premature RBC destruction)
- Acute or chronic blood loss
- Aplastic anaemia
- Anaemia of chronic disease (kidney disease, chronic inflammation)

Notes:
- A mild physiological decline in HGB during pregnancy (100–110 g/L) is normal due to increased plasma volume.
- HGB < 70 g/L generally warrants consideration of a blood transfusion — seek medical attention promptly.
- Combine with MCV and MCH to classify the type of anaemia and guide treatment.
