Indicator: MCH (Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin)
Full name: Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin

Reference range: 26 – 34 pg
Unit: pg (picograms)

Clinical significance:
MCH is the average mass of hemoglobin contained in a single red blood cell. It helps classify anaemia alongside MCV, indicating whether red cells are well-filled ("hyperchromic") or poorly filled ("hypochromic") with hemoglobin.

HIGH (hyperchromic, MCH > 34 pg) — common causes:
- Megaloblastic anaemia (vitamin B12 or folate deficiency)
- Liver disease
- Hypothyroidism

LOW (hypochromic, MCH < 26 pg) — common causes:
- Iron-deficiency anaemia (most common)
- Thalassaemia
- Sideroblastic anaemia
- Anaemia of chronic blood loss

Notes:
- MCH and MCV almost always move in the same direction (both high or both low).
- A low MCH means each red cell carries less hemoglobin than normal — the cells appear paler under a microscope (hypochromia).
- MCH in isolation has limited clinical value; always evaluate alongside MCHC, MCV, and iron studies for a complete picture.
